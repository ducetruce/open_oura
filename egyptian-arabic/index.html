How Egyptian sounds differ

Egyptian Arabic (مصري, masri) is the most widely understood dialect in the Arab world, thanks to Egyptian film and TV. It is written with the same letters as Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), but several letters are said differently.

Grammar in five rules

  • Present tense takes b-. bashrab “I drink”, bitishrab “you drink”.
  • Future takes ha-. hashrab “I will drink”.
  • Negate verbs with ma-…-sh. mabashrabsh “I don't drink”. Negate everything else with mish: mish 3aarif “I don't know”.
  • Question words go at the end. ismak eh? “your name what?”, el-7ammaam feen? “the bathroom where?”
  • “This” follows the noun. el-kitaab da “this book”, el-3arabeyya di “this car”.

Talking to men and women

Many words change for the person you address. Cards mark these with m / f. Adding -a usually makes the feminine: 3aayiz → 3ayza (want), aasif → asfa (sorry). Addressing a woman, -ak becomes -ik: ezzayak → ezzayik.
